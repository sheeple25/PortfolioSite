Design has a homogenisation problem. It flattens local particularity into global scalability, and it imports its picture of the future from somewhere else. This thesis asks whether Indian science fiction — the oldest non-Western SF canon — can be formalised into a design methodology that works against that flattening.

The output is a framework, and a website that runs it. What follows is the argument in condensed form; every section opens further if you want the long version.

## The Problem

Preview:

> "the nineteenth century dream of one world … haunts us with the prospect of a fully homogenized, technologically controlled, absolutely hierarchized world"

*Nandy, 1985*

Four pathologies run through design as it is dominantly practiced:

**The Homogenization Mandate.** Design erases local particularity in the name of global scalability.

**The Fiction of Neutrality.** We design for a generic "user" — and silently replicate the hierarchies we ignore.

**Complicity in Extraction.** Design optimises for engagement and transaction, not ecological or social limits.

**The Outsourced Imagination.** Our collective vision of the future is not cultivated. It is imported.

Downstream of these sit three crises: a crisis of **imagination**, a crisis of relevance, and a crisis of agency.

This is flattening.

[NOTE imagination: The flattening isn't aesthetic — it's epistemological. It shapes what futures we can imagine, not just how things look. Which is why the response has to be epistemic relocation rather than simply more design.]

![Four mandates and three crises](diagram:fig-0-3-1 "Fig 0. The four mandates and the three crises they produce")

Expanded:

The four mandates are not separate failures. They compound. Designing for a generic user (neutrality) is what makes a design scalable across markets (homogenisation), which is what makes extraction efficient, which requires a future imported from wherever the capital already is.

The proposed response is not more design. It is *epistemic relocation* — moving outside the Eurocentric design episteme rather than reforming it from within. That move needs somewhere to go, and this research argues that Indian science fiction is a place with the structural properties required.

## The Proposition

Preview:

Three claims, in sequence:

**[1]** Science fiction is contextually critical.

**[2]** Indian SF, rooted in a postcolonial, non-Western empirical environment, offers critique that is structurally unavailable in Western speculative and critical design.

**[3]** We can use Indian SF narratives as sites for speculative design — and in doing so, **unflatten**.

[NOTE unflatten: The claim about Indian SF being structurally variegated is methodological, not political. Banerjee (2020) describes a canon that neither wholly mirrors nor rejects Western influence — it operates in productive tension, and that tension is the usable property.]

Expanded:

### The research question

> "How can the latent design logics within Indian Science Fiction be formalized into a critical methodology for reimagining the practice, pedagogy, and purpose of design, generating culturally coherent alternatives to its global, homogenized present?"

Guided by five subquestions, which are lenses operating simultaneously rather than sequential steps:

**[1] Analysis.** How does design manifest within each selected narrative — through what objects, interfaces or systems, and what do they imply about the world?

**[2] Extrapolation.** What new forms of designer agency and expertise are required for this world to function?

**[3] Briefcraft.** How can a functional design brief be generated from within this world logic, using its constraints as creative parameters?

**[4] Testing.** How can the core proposition of such a brief be materialized through prototype or scenario?

**[5] Synthesis.** What does this process reveal about current design frameworks and the potential for culturally specific practices?

Subquestions 1–3 map onto the three framework stages — story analysis, profile making, brief making. Four and five are testing and reflection.

### Methodology

The research oscillates between theory and practice, not as alternating steps but simultaneously: every chapter carries both narrative work (what the story gives) and research grounding (what theory says about it).

![Oscillation between narrative work and theoretical grounding](diagram:fig-0-4-1 "Fig. Methodology: The oscillation between narrative work and theoretical grounding")

## Science Fiction as a Design Site

Preview:

Suvin's definition is the one this research works from:

> "SF is, then, a literary genre whose necessary and sufficient conditions are the presence and interaction of estrangement and cognition, and whose main formal device is an imaginative framework alternative to the author's empirical environment"

*Suvin, 1979, pp.7–8*

Which reduces to: **SF = novum + cognitive estrangement**.

[NOTE novum: A novum is "a totalizing phenomenon or relationship deviating from the author's and implied reader's norm of reality" (Suvin, 1979, p.64). Not merely a striking gadget — it must totalise the world. Le Guin's Left Hand of Darkness has one novum, ambisexuality, and it renders every gender-based social structure visible by removing it.]

[NOTE cognitive estrangement, estrangement: Estrangement must be cognitive — it must allow rational comparison between the fictional world and the reader's normative assumptions. This is what separates SF from fantasy: you don't merely find it strange, you are forced to think about your own reality against it.]

What is considered strange is defined by what is considered normal. A mobile phone is SF in 1800 and mundane in 2026 — which is why SF is always a document of its own moment.

Expanded:

### Speculative and critical design

> "Design speculations can act as a catalyst for collectively redefining our relationship to reality."

*Dunne & Raby, 2013, p.02*

SCD offers three tools:

**Fictional worlds.** "fictional worlds are inherently speculative … have the potential to form sites for design. Of particular interest … the use of the ideas in the background, and not so much the story or plot themselves."

**Thought experiments.** *Reductio ad absurdum* and counterfactuals — speculation with one or few variables, meant to think about particular issues in isolation.

**What-ifs.** Forward-looking. "They unpick where, why, and how things could break down or go wrong."

The methodological move this research makes is narrow but consequential: replace "fictional worlds in general" with "Indian SF narratives specifically."

![Dunne and Raby's A/B manifesto](diagram:fig-1-4-1 "Fig 1. Dunne and Raby's A/B manifesto")

### The Indian landscape

Indian SF is the oldest non-Western canon. Its first entry — Kylas Chunder Dutt, 1835 — predates Jules Verne by thirty years. Key names include Vandana Singh, Anil Menon and Tarun K. Saint, alongside popular cinema milestones like Ra.One, PK and Kalki 2898 AD which mark cultural presence without being the kind of SF this framework works with.

SCD, by contrast, is thin on the ground:

> "As a formalised design practice, SCD appears relatively underdeveloped in India, appearing in places through pockets of pedagogy."

Practitioners are scattered — CEPT University, LMNO, Superflux, IIT IDC. The sparseness is both the problem and the opportunity; this research is a contribution to that consolidation.

There is also hermeneutical friction at the level of language. *Science* and *vijnana* are not the same concept, and that gap is epistemological rather than merely translational.

> "an evolving relationship between Indian culture and modernity and science will inevitably alter the way that SF is conceived in India"

*Banerjee, 2020, p.07*

![The Indian SF and SCD landscape](diagram:fig-1-6-1 "Fig 2. The Indian SF and SCD landscape")

## Choosing the Stories

Preview:

> "The Indian SF landscape is one which has only recently begun to be seriously mapped. This means there is not, as of yet, as comprehensive a genealogy as is available for Western SF."

The corpus selected was **The Gollancz Book of South Asian Science Fiction**, Vols. 1 and 2, edited by Tarun K. Saint — 60+ short stories, genre-faithful, with no thematic restriction inside SF. Saint frames it explicitly as a **mapping exercise** for the field.

From those, three worlds were selected:

- **The Ministry of Relevance** — governance and surveillance
- **Shit Flower** — social fabric and relationality
- **The Diamond Library** — knowledge control

[NOTE mapping exercise: The phrase matters. The corpus was assembled to represent the field, not to argue a position within it — which is exactly the property a framework needing formal variance requires.]

Expanded:

### What was rejected, and why

**The Blaft Book of Anti-Caste Science Fiction (2024).** A collection explicitly pre-filtering on works that address caste and anti-caste voice "as an act of political correction." Stories are selected for what they argue, not for their quality as SF. The selection criteria is not formal but ideological — and this framework requires thematic variance, so an ideologically curated collection cannot be representative.

**Multispecies Cities: Solarpunk Urban Futures (2021).** An anthology with a specific editorial focus on more-than-human kinship in a solarpunk urban setting. Same concern as Blaft: the strong thematic focus means the stories are not representative but niche. Thematic restriction is incompatible with a framework requiring formal variety.

Neither rejection is a value judgment. Both collections do important cultural work. The issue is fit.

### Scoring

Stories were assessed on four weighted criteria — Novum (40%), Degree of Design (30%), Worldbuilding (20%), Social Architecture (10%) — scored High/Medium/Low as 3/2/1 and summed.

The average row is the interesting one: highest on social architecture, lowest on degree of design. That is Banerjee's observation about the canon made empirical. The three selections scored 27, 29 and 29 — the highest scorers within their respective groups rather than the highest overall.

### Plot structure as argument

Each story's narrative structure carries its argument:

- **The Ministry of Relevance** is a sled down a hill — deterministic, helpless. Once Bhaskar walks into The Locus there is no escape, and the reader knows it.
- **Shit Flower** is nonlinear, running past and present chronologies side by side.
- **The Diamond Library** is nested loops of truth; the protagonist spirals inward, and discovery is loss of ground rather than gain.

### The Reading List

Six texts hold up the theoretical frame — SF, SCD and ontological design, read through Suvin, Dunne & Raby, Willis, Fry, Escobar and Banerjee — alongside the four anthology covers behind the corpus decision above: both Gollancz volumes, and the two collections rejected for being curated on theme rather than form.

## How Design Designs

Preview:

> "Ontological designing, then, is a hermeneutics of design concerned with the nature and agency of design, which understands design as a subject-decentred practice, acknowledging that things as well as people design"

*Willis, 2006, p.70*

**Ontological design** in three steps:

**01** All things are designed, either directly or indirectly.

**02** The creator of a thing influences its creation — designs it.

**03** The design of a thing in turn influences, *designs*, all downstream interaction, beyond even the intent of the creator.

And the cycle continues — each new thing is designed in a world already shaped by what came before.

[NOTE ontological design: Nobody picks up a knife and thinks about cutting. The relationship is so embedded it is invisible. That is the point — ontological design is about what has become invisible. The three steps are not a process, they are a description of how the world already works, with or without our awareness of it.]

Expanded:

### Designing consequences

Karl Benz did not intend to design traffic law, oil wars, suburban sprawl or car subcultures. He intended to design an engine. The gap between intention and consequence is what ontological design names.

This is the argument for why designers cannot treat their work as bounded. The brief ends at handoff. The designing does not.

### Defuturing

> "we are now at a point when it can no longer be assumed that we, en masse, have a future. If we do, it can only be by design against the still accelerating defuturing condition of unsustainability … as it acts to take futures away from ourselves and other living species"

*Fry, 2009, p.1*

**Defuturing** happens in every action taken — the elimination of possible futures that might have come to pass had a different action been taken. At civilisational scale, our collective priorities actively move futures from probable to plausible, and from plausible to merely possible.

Design, as it is dominantly practiced, is defuturing.

[NOTE defuturing, defuture: Defuturing is a diagnostic, not pessimism. If design can defuture it can also refuture — narrowing the futures cone, or widening it. That reversal is the entire premise of speculative and critical design.]

![The futures cone, narrowed by defuturing](diagram:fig-3-5-1 "Fig 5. The futures cone, narrowed by defuturing")

## The Framework

Preview:

> "The ultimate intention of this exploration is to create a framework which simultaneously performs two equally important functions."

**01 Procedural expansion.** Extending Indian SF narratives into sites for critique through speculative design.

**02 Situated practice.** Creating varied, unique and provocative contexts for designers, educators and students to develop the skill of inhabiting speculative realities — and designing for them through repetition.

The two functions are not sequential. Every output is both a procedural step and a design context in its own right.

![The framework end to end](diagram:fig-proj-overview "Fig 3. Project Overview: The framework end to end")

Expanded:

### Why profiles, why briefs

**Profiles.** If the world designs the designer, then the designer is an artefact of their world. The profile is the instrument for reading that artefact — understanding the world through the practitioners it produces.

**Briefs.** If the designer must act in the world, they need a commission. The brief is the interface between the world's logic and the inhabiting designer's action — it is not *about* the world, it is *of* it.

Profile = world read inward. Brief = world pushed outward. Together they close the loop.

The underlying structure is a doubled double diamond: diverge to understand the world and converge into a profile; diverge to find problems and converge into a brief. Profile and brief are not independent outputs — each profile constrains the brief, because you can only commission something that makes sense for the designer that world produced.

![The doubled double diamond](diagram:fig-double-diamond "Fig 4. The doubled double diamond")

### Background instruments

Before either stage runs, three instruments establish the world: a **world timeline** across twelve domains with a chosen centre of gravity; a **world epistemology** diagnostic; and **stakeholder mapping** built on Latour's actor-network theory, which is what allows machines, institutions and non-human agents to count as stakeholders.

Each world's centre of gravity explains why its profiles and briefs look so different — governance and surveillance for the Ministry of Relevance, social fabric for Shit Flower, knowledge control for The Diamond Library.

## Reading a World Through Its Designers

Preview:

The argument is a chain:

**[1]** Design, as it is dominantly practiced, is defuturing — designers are emitters of change, and their practice shapes what can and cannot come next.

**[2]** "We come to be what we are not just genetically prefigured but equally prefigured by the particular designing character of the artificially fabricated world into which we arrive." *(Fry, 2009, p.24)* — the world designs the designer.

**[3]** "In the same manner a fossil may be analysed for its composition, from which we glean the environment and behaviour in which it was created, so can a designer be seen as an artefact of the world which created it."

The designer is therefore a **diagnostic** of the world.

[NOTE diagnostic: The profile is not a biography. It is a trace fossil — you read the world from its marks. This is why profiles come before briefs: you need to know what a world produces before you can commission from inside it.]

Expanded:

### L1 and L2

**L1** designers are extracted from the nova — they are the creators of the totalising phenomenon. Their relationship with the novum is the signal: not just who they are, but the act of making itself. L1 designers come from canon, not from speculation.

**L2** designers are constructed in the story-world the nova produced. They are artefacts of the changed world, not its architects, generated by speculation from the conditions the nova created.

The condition for being L2 is binary: exists after nova. There is no L3.

This is not a quality ranking — both are necessary. L1 is more grounded, L2 more generative. In the Ministry of Relevance: the designer of the Relevance Score is L1; a compliance consultant navigating Scores daily is L2.

![The L1 / L2 distinction](diagram:fig-4-3-1 "Fig 6. The L1 / L2 distinction")

### Profile format

> "What details must be decided upon in order for a designer to be considered 'created'?"

Six components:

**01 Ideology / bio** — what the designer believes design is for.

**02 Education** — a guaranteed touchpoint between designer and world-pedagogy.

**03 Experience** — situates the designer in the formal context of the world.

**04 Skills** — an artefact of what the story-world values; the market tells you what is wanted.

**05 Day in the life** — imagining a day develops the world through its interfaces and affordances.

**06 Artefacts** — visualisations of key moments; depth through visual registers and naming.

The CV is used as the format deliberately. It is inherently an indictment of the market — what makes it onto the profile, and how it is presented, is already an artefact of the world. Two dimensions go undeclared and do a great deal of work: the headshot (fashion, body modification, world aesthetics) and the **name**.

[NOTE name, naming: The Tiffany Problem — "Tiffany" is a genuinely medieval name, but using it in a medieval setting breaks immersion because readers don't believe it. The same applies forwards: a 2089 designer shouldn't carry a 2026 job title.]

![The designer profile template](diagram:fig-4-3-2 "Fig 7. The designer profile template")

## Commissioning From Inside a World

Preview:

> "A design brief … provides a road map for the entire design process, and an invaluable tool for securing project approval at the end of the process." *(Philips, 2004, p.15)*

> "The ability to convey not only the stated intention of the issuing entity but also their unstated, unsaid intention is the true strength of the design brief as a diegetic prototype. The document itself is the impression of the issuer."

> "Any ethical friction, reflection or pause is produced by the reader or world-inhabitor in their own perception, and is completely ignored in the document itself because it is a reflection of a world that is ignoring such questions."

That last one is the **Institution Sincerity Principle**, and it is what makes the brief critical.

[NOTE Institution Sincerity Principle, Institution Sincerity: The commissioning body cannot name its own harm. The brief must be earnest, unapologetic and internally coherent — the critique happens in the reader, not in the document. Think of Minority Report: preventative policing framed as a neutral design problem. The institution never considered "is it acceptable to arrest someone for something that hasn't happened yet?" to be a question at all.]

Expanded:

### Problem extraction

> "'Problem' is in itself a relational term, because what is a problem for one may not be a problem for another."

Six diagnostic lenses:

**01 Goal obstruction** — what prevents the stakeholder from achieving their core goal?

**02 Relationship friction** — do relationships with other stakeholders create stress or breakdown?

**03 Epistemic blind spots** — do problems arise from what they naturalise or take for granted?

**04 Systemic constraint** — do systems or infrastructure constrain their operational space?

**05 Naming constraints / unspeakables** — what is needed or wanted but cannot be stated openly?

**06 Timeline positioning** — problems are temporal; situate them in the world timeline.

Two passes run in order, and the order matters. Pass A finds world gaps phenomenologically, by inhabiting the story as a reader. Pass B finds stakeholder problems analytically. Once you are inside the stakeholder frame you stop seeing the gaps, so A must precede B.

The problem area is relational — it exists from the perspective of a stakeholder, and the issuing body need not be the affected one.

![The brief creation process](diagram:fig-5-3-2 "Fig 8. The brief creation process")

### Briefcraft

Four questions for inhabiting the issuer:

**01** What problem harms this institution the most in their estimation?

**02** What does the issuing institution see when it sees this problem?

**03** What does the institution believe it needs?

**04** Who is benefiting from this? How does this institution frame this?

These are not a checklist. They are an exercise in inhabiting a perspective that is not your own, and may be one you find morally objectionable.

Structure comes from **5W + H** — who, what, when, where, why, how — answering problem statement, context, stakeholders, constraints, deliverables and timeframe. Order and specificity are deliberately unfixed: maximum freedom produces maximum variance in briefs.

Format is part of the diegesis. RFP, community notice, government circular, conversational brief — form and tone are world-signals. Default templates are forbidden, because they prevent briefs from existing as authentic representations of their context. Word choice is an artefact too: each institution has a vocabulary defined by its objective, both actual and stated.

### Assessing a brief

Two clusters. **Extraction** — story specificity, speculative demand, defuturing — reveals the world's design logic. **Immersion** — causal grounding, diegetic authority — maintains world-internal coherence. Both must be present.

The test is backward inference: read only the constraints. If you cannot reconstruct *this specific world* from them alone — not a type of world, but this one — the brief is too safe.

Brief quality as a design document matters less than diegetic integrity. A well-written brief that is diegetically false fails the matrix; a clunky but honest one passes.

## Testing It

Preview:

> "Primary purpose: gather preliminary evidence — not verification — that the methodology occasions critical reflection in practicing designers."

Five participants, each assigned a world and a brief, working in a café on personal laptops with their own LLM accounts. Most had not slept; this is acknowledged as a confounding factor.

The success criterion was not output quality. It was critical reflection connecting the speculative world to real design practice or real institutional structures. **All five** produced it.

[NOTE All five: The engagement arc was consistent across every participant — reluctant, then cognitively burdened, then immersed, then tired but satisfied — with the shift into absorbed design engagement at roughly the forty-minute mark.]

Expanded:

### Setup

Three goals were set. Two were met; the third — gathering feedback on the website as a facilitation tool — was deferred, because participants did not use the site and it requires a separate walkthrough session.

Participants and assignments:

- **Tejasvini** — Ministry of Relevance, B-04 Civic Movement Infrastructure
- **Nipun** — The Diamond Library, Surface Experience
- **Swathi** — Shit Flower, Reliquary for Past Selves
- **Ishani** — Shit Flower, Antariksh Samskara
- **Divija** — Ministry of Relevance, B-03 Vision Perpetuation Framework

Worlds were assigned by the researcher rather than self-selected, controlling for brief content and participant background.

![Testing session structure](diagram:fig-test-overview "Fig 9. Testing session structure")

### Findings

**Engagement arc.** Reluctant → cognitively burdened → immersed → tired but satisfied. Consistent across all five, with the shift at approximately forty minutes.

**"Fun."** The word participants reached for most often to describe both the briefs and the exercise. Unprompted, consistent, and significant.

**Brief splitting.** Invoked organically, universally well received, described as essential for making brief scope workable. The single most practically successful element of the session.

**The spectrum.** Compliance (Divija) → humanistic inflection (Ishani) → active subversion (Tejasvini). The brief does not produce a uniform response, which is consistent with its intent.

What follows from that:

- The diegetic frame functions as a critical irritant even under adverse conditions.
- The engagement threshold carries a cost. The difficulty may be structural to the mechanism — not a problem to smooth over, but something onboarding should acknowledge directly.
- The LLM oracle constraint does not hold by default. Conditioning toward LLM-as-collaborator is strong, and must be surfaced to participants before design work begins rather than merely embedded in the instructions.
- The strongest reflections were world-to-real-practice connections, not abstract ethical claims. The speculative frame produced reflection by analogy, not by abstraction.

### The outputs

All five outputs are diegetically plausible, and the medium was self-selected and varied — institutional document, garment system, systems diagram, working notes, product plus spatial sequence. The brief format is genuinely medium-agnostic.

**Divija — Ministry of Relevance.** An institutional document in the same format as the brief. Four solutions: legally empowered relics, the leader's tears as mandatory eye drops, a DNA-seeded tree declared the leader's living body, and leader DNA distributed through national water and soil. The most diegetically coherent output — fully inhabited the commissioning logic without resistance, and genuinely disturbing in institutional voice.

![Divija's output — Vision Perpetuation Framework](/projects/TestingDivija.png?w=913&h=1200 "Fig 10. Divija's output — Vision Perpetuation Framework")

**Nipun — The Diamond Library.** A "First Exposure Visor," fully component-labelled: impact shell, aperture fins, visor layer, air filtration unit, neck brace. Alongside it a five-stage spatial onboarding sequence — Containment → Transition Awareness → Release → Adapt → Exposure. The aperture fins are the most sophisticated decision: controllable gradation of light, managing the rate of encounter rather than blocking it. The only participant who designed both an object and the space containing it — and he had misread the task entirely before being oriented. Onboarding failure does not predict output failure.

![Nipun's output — First Exposure Visor and onboarding sequence](placeholder:TestingNipun.png "Fig 11. Nipun's output — First Exposure Visor and onboarding sequence")

**Ishani — Shit Flower.** A fully conceived transition garment system: ritual procedure, garment construction logic, material decisions, sizing, step-by-step sequence. Actively designed *against* the world's implicit caste logic — accessibility over exclusivity, noted explicitly. The clearest example of a designer inflecting a world's logic with humanistic values without rejecting the commissioning frame.

![Ishani's output — Antariksh Samskara](/projects/TestingIshani.png?w=2380&h=1200 "Fig 12. Ishani's output — Antariksh Samskara")

**Tejasvini — Ministry of Relevance.** A systems diagram working through two approaches to movement reduction, with "RESTORE AUTHENTIC HUMAN CONNECTION" circled and annotated with "why" — the clearest visual evidence of critical reflection in any output. She also explored a second brief independently. Subversive engagement is not disengagement.

![Tejasvini's output — Civic Movement Infrastructure](/projects/TestingTejasvini.png?w=2108&h=1200 "Fig 13. Tejasvini's output — Civic Movement Infrastructure")

**Swathi — Shit Flower.** Working notes: philosophically dense, formally underresolved. A tripartite framework — what is shared between Nuk and Pama, what is truly Nuk's, what is not Nuk's — and two candidate objects. "Allow the gap to exist / make that unrecognisable okay to be with / coexist" is the most philosophically sophisticated language in any output, reframing the brief's problem from resolution to accommodation. Her discomfort that a speculative world could contain a "correct" design direction is itself a finding; she had assumed speculative space implied unlimited interpretive freedom.

Notably, the LLM adjudicated her interpretation as incorrect when her reading was arguably the more nuanced one — a direct failure of the StoryGuide constraint set.

![Swathi's output — Reliquary for Past Selves](/projects/TestingSwathi.png?w=868&h=1200 "Fig 14. Swathi's output — Reliquary for Past Selves")

## What I Learned

Preview:

**[1]** "In the real world, we are all L2 designers operating in an environment irrevocably altered by totalising novums of the past — the mobile phone, the internet, social media." Design designs. This is not a speculative claim; it is the condition we already inhabit.

**[2]** "In the same manner a brief from the autocratic MoR belies the sinister undertones of that world and the underlying wish to control the populace, so do we see the same in real life." Building the diegetic framework was a lesson in reading briefs in the real world.

**[3]** SF, SCD and ontological design inevitably feed into each other in a sort of **perichoresis**.

[NOTE perichoresis: Greek perichōrēsis — "rotation, going around" — originally used for the mutual indwelling of the Trinity. Three distinct entities dwelling within one another, each constituting the others without losing its own identity. Remove any one of the three and the framework collapses.]

Expanded:

### Limitations

**Corpus language.** "This research has engaged with a limited subset of the field — English language anthologies … To truly be able to extricate the design logic from non-English works, there should ideally be study in their original language." Given the framework's own argument about epistemic relocation, this is the more serious of the two limitations.

**Web infrastructure.** "The site is almost feature-complete visually … but there were many more technical aspects to making the website 'live' … that were outside the scope of this research."

There is also a genuine accessibility problem with The Diamond Library: a speculative distance of 30,000 years is not adequately bridged by a 200–300 word entry report. Far-future worlds need extended onboarding.

### What comes next

**01** Web infrastructure and backend — user authorisation, servers, LLM API access — so designers can submit to the living corpus, and each submission expands the canon.

**02** Non-English works, and an update to the framework if its procedural logic needs adjusting to accommodate them.

> "whatever future — good or bad — that lays ahead will be more definitively our own if it is not imported from elsewhere and instead stems from our localised present"
