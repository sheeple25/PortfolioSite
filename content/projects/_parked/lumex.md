A short material study, and a question: what happens if you braid a textile around something that isn't cloth?

Reinforcement inside an already durable braid should make a textile that carries load and keeps its form. That would put textiles into jobs they currently can't do.

## The Reference Object

The idea came from a desk lamp's neck.

It does two things at once — bears the weight of the bulb, and stays exactly where you leave it. Bring both properties to a textile and the material stops being a surface.

So the test case became an adaptive braided lamp for student desks.

## What to Braid Around

Two variables: the cord, and the core.

**Cords**, most to least flexible: nylon, polypropylene, cotton.

**Cores** are the interesting part, and they split on a property that isn't stiffness.

Stiff metals — steel, brass — are elastic. Bend them and they spring back to where they were. Useless here.

Flexible metals are plastic. Bend them and they stay bent. That, not strength, is the property the gooseneck depends on.

Springiness is the enemy of adjustability. The best core is a metal that gives up and stays put.

## Trade-offs in the Weave

Two ways to reinforce, each costing something.

**Homogenous reinforcement** distributes strength evenly and uses more material.

**Continuous reinforcement** runs the core through unbroken, and shows on the surface finish.

Leaving space for an electrical cable keeps the wire enclosed and leaves a visible gap. Closing the gap gives a clean surface and warps when bent.

No combination wins outright. The choice depends on whether the piece is meant to be looked at or handled.

## The Shade

For the shade the braid needed to be rigid rather than adjustable, so resin was used as a binder — producing a thin, light, stiff surface from the same braided cloth as the flexible neck.

One technique, two opposite behaviours, decided entirely by what it's bound with.

## Where It Got To

Two iterations, at part level — a flexible neck with an enclosed cable sleeve, and a resin-bound shade.

It stayed a material study rather than becoming a product. The finding it produced is the elasticity/plasticity split: with a plastic core, a braided textile can hold a bent form under load, which is the property the whole idea depended on.
