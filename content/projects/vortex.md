Ply-split braiding is a rare textile technique from rural Rajasthan. Any cord of fabric or other ductile material can be intertwined into a textile by hand.

It is versatile, breathable, and strong in tension. The brief was to find it a functional application.

## Betting on Tension

Of the three properties, tensile strength was the one nothing else was using.

That pointed at **tensegrity** — a principle where a discontinuous set of compression elements is balanced by a tensile force, stabilising the whole structure.

The T3 prism is the simplest form of it. And it happens to look like a stool.

So: replace the three topmost tensional members of a T3 prism with a single braided surface. The textile stops being upholstery laid over a frame and becomes a structural member. It holds the frame together, and you sit on it.

## Finding the Structure

Five braided structures, tested against each other: edge seal, darning, SCOT, POT, corner seal.

Software simulation isolated the high-wear areas — the corners where the legs meet the seat — and I chained different structures together, using each where its properties fit.

The chaining worked. The seat didn't.

## The First One Failed

A tight structure around a stretchy centre. Structurally sound, and it felt like sitting on three wires.

The load path was correct and the experience was wrong, which is a specific kind of failure: nothing in the simulation could have caught it.

**Thinner cord, tighter and more uniform throughout.** Losing the stiff-edge/soft-middle contrast spread the load across the whole surface instead of channelling it down three lines. The second iteration was comfortable.

## The Prototype

560 × 560 × 580mm. Three-ply cotton cord for the seat, solid teak legs, galvanised steel chain as the tension members.

3kg of chair, carrying around 120kg of person.

## What I Learned

How to design around a technique and its properties, rather than picking a technique to execute a form.

Also patience. Ply-split braiding is done by hand, one pass at a time, and there is no way to make that go faster.
